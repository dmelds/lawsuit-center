Who This Page May Help

Situations people often research.

Peritoneal mesothelioma starts in the lining of the abdomen. It is less common than the type that forms around the lungs, and the exposure behind it is often harder to pin down. These histories come up most in a review.

  • Belly swelling, abdominal pain or fluid buildup that led to the diagnosis
  • No clear memory of ever working around asbestos
  • Long-term use of talcum powder or baby powder
  • Dust brought home on a parent's or spouse's work clothes
  • Navy service, shipyard work, or a job in refining, power or construction
  • A relative who died after a peritoneal mesothelioma diagnosis

Timing

Why the diagnosis date matters.

Peritoneal mesothelioma is usually confirmed by a biopsy of the abdominal lining, sometimes during surgery. The date on that pathology report is the one most states use to start the filing clock.

The exposure may have happened decades before, and that is expected. What matters is acting soon after the diagnosis, because the time allowed is short. When the person has died, the deadline usually counts from the date of death instead. A lawyer can confirm which date applies.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions.

Do I need to know where the asbestos came from?

No. Many people with peritoneal mesothelioma never knowingly worked with asbestos. Lawyers piece together the history from jobs, homes, military service and products used over the years. Tell us whatever you remember.

Can I request a review for a family member who died?

Yes. Select the option for a loved one who has passed and include the date of death if you know it. That date often starts a separate filing deadline.

What if the exposure may have come from talcum powder?

Select talc or baby powder as the exposure source. Many lawsuits say cosmetic talc was contaminated with asbestos, and some involve peritoneal mesothelioma. The manufacturers deny it, and juries have ruled both ways.

Is there a cost to request a review?

No cost and no obligation. Firms that handle mesothelioma typically take a percentage of any recovery and charge nothing if there is none.
